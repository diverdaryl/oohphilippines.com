Media Buying 101

Why billboards deserve a spot on your media plan

CPM (cost per mille) is how advertisers compare media prices across completely different channels: it's the cost to put your message in front of 1,000 people. The lower the CPM, the more eyeballs every peso buys.

Channel Typical CPM*
🏙️ Major billboard (this platform) ₱15–60
📱 Facebook / Instagram ads₱150–400+
▶️ YouTube pre-roll₱200–500
📺 National TV spot₱300+

*Billboard range computed from operator-quoted rates and modeled OTS reach on OOHPhilippines.com. Digital and TV figures are indicative industry ranges — actual prices vary by targeting, season, and negotiation.

Why the gap in OOH's favor:
🚫 Can't be skipped, blocked, or scrolled past — a billboard is 100% viewable, on duty 24/7
🔁 Frequency is built in — the same commuters pass your site daily, and repetition builds brands
🌆 Real-world presence — you reach people out where they shop, drive, and decide
🏆 Instant credibility — a large physical placement signals an established brand
📈 Multiplies your digital spend — people who see a brand outdoors are more likely to search for and engage with it online

The honest fine print: billboard reach is broad, not targeted, and nobody can click it. That's exactly why smart media plans pair OOH's cheap mass reach with digital's precision — rather than choosing one or the other.

How to pick your numbers

Making sensible assumptions

Billboards can't be clicked, so ROI is modeled, not tracked. These three numbers are your hypothesis about the campaign — here's how to set each one:

👀 Attention rate — of everyone who passes, who actually notices? A huge lit board at a slow, congested choke point deserves 25–35%. A mid-size board on a fast, cluttered stretch: 10–15%. Simple, bold creative earns the higher end. Default 20% is a fair middle.
🛒 Conversion rate — of those who noticed, who eventually buys? This depends on your product more than the billboard: everyday low-price products (fast food, load, drinks) 0.5–2%; considered purchases (appliances, clinics, banks) 0.1–0.5%; big-ticket (cars, condos) 0.01–0.05% — tiny, but each sale is huge. A billboard near your actual store converts far better than a general image site.
💵 Average transaction value — your average receipt. For repeat-purchase products, using first-purchase value is the conservative case; a new customer's lifetime value is the upside case.

The professional's trick — work backwards: don't predict, gut-check. The green box under your results tells you how few buyers are needed to break even. If "1 in 4,000 people who noticed" sounds easily believable for your product, the campaign likely pays for itself.
