Outdoor Advertising in the Philippines: Formats, Costs & How to Start

Published 2026  ·  OOH Philippines  ·  8 min read

Outdoor advertising — also called out-of-home (OOH) advertising or outdoor media — is any advertising a person encounters while outside their home: billboards, LED boards, transit ads, and street furniture. In the Philippines, it's one of the most visible and highest-reach advertising channels available, and one of the few forms of media that traffic-heavy cities like Metro Manila can't help but notice.

This guide covers what outdoor advertising includes, what it costs, and how to actually book a campaign.

What Counts as Outdoor Advertising?

"Outdoor advertising" and "OOH" are used interchangeably in the Philippines, though OOH is the more common industry term. Both refer to the same set of formats:

  • Static billboards — printed tarpaulin or vinyl on a fixed structure. The most common and affordable outdoor ad format.
  • LED / digital billboards (DOOH) — screens that rotate digital creative, often shared across multiple advertisers in a loop.
  • Lamp post banners — smaller vertical banners mounted along a corridor, typically sold in bulk sets for saturation coverage.
  • Transit ads — ads on buses, jeepneys, MRT/LRT stations, and terminals.
  • Street furniture and mall displays — bus shelters, mall standees, and indoor/outdoor digital screens in high-footfall venues.

Billboards remain the dominant format by spend and visibility, which is why "outdoor advertising" and "billboard advertising" are often used to mean the same thing in everyday conversation — even though outdoor media technically covers more ground.

Why Outdoor Advertising Works in the Philippines

A few conditions make the Philippines an unusually strong market for outdoor media:

Heavy Traffic, Long Exposure

Metro Manila's traffic is notorious — but for outdoor advertisers, long commute times mean repeated exposure. A commuter stuck on EDSA passes the same billboard multiple times a day, reinforcing recall far more than a single glance would.

High Time Spent Outside the Home

Filipinos spend significant time in malls, markets, churches, and public transit — all environments where outdoor ads are unavoidable, unlike TV or streaming ads that can be skipped or muted.

Lower Clutter Outside Metro Manila

Provincial cities like Iloilo, Bacolod, and Cagayan de Oro have far fewer competing billboards than Manila, meaning a single well-placed structure can dominate local brand recall.

How to Book Outdoor Advertising in the Philippines

1. Go Direct to an Operator

Contact billboard and outdoor media companies directly. This works if you already know which operator has inventory in your target area — but requires separate outreach and negotiation for each one.

2. Work Through a Media Buying Agency

Agencies handle placement across multiple operators and can negotiate volume rates, typically for a 15–20% commission.

3. Search a Billboard Directory

OOH Philippines lets you search outdoor advertising inventory from operators across the country in one place — by city, corridor, and format — before contacting anyone. See our full billboard directory guide for details.

Tip: Most operators require a minimum booking of one month for static billboards, with better rates unlocked at 6 or 12-month commitments.

Outdoor Advertising vs. Digital Advertising

Outdoor advertising and digital ads aren't competitors so much as complements. Outdoor builds broad, unavoidable brand awareness across a physical market; digital allows precise targeting and measurable performance. Many Philippine brands run outdoor for reach and recall, then retarget or reinforce the same audience through digital and social.
